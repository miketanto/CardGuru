What the net sees

The agent's entire view of a Magic battlefield is ten numbers. Here is what they are, what they throw away, and why that hasn't cost us anything measurable yet — which is the part that decides what to fix.

What that costs

Counts and sums are not a board. Six genuinely different boards from our own card pool produce one identical vector — the net cannot tell them apart, and they play nothing alike.

Why it hasn't hurt yet

The net never has to reconstruct the board, because the options arrive pre-resolved. Every candidate is an afterstate: CombatMath simulates the subset against a best-replying defender and hands over the consequence.

The trap in that. The afterstate only rescues us for questions answerable inside one combat. A k-turn value depends on which creatures survive, not how much total power does — and the candidate is aggregate there too: retainedBodies, retainedPower, retainedToughness. The moment the objective looks past this combat, both channels fail together.

The vocabulary already exists. e2_extract.py emits 68 mechanical features per card — including flags for Flying, Deathtouch, First Strike, Trample, Vigilance, Menace, Ward, Hexproof and eleven more, plus tgt_creature / tgt_player_or_any / tgt_spell. It is passed to identity() for single-card candidates and never to encodeState. A 2/2 Flier and a 2/2 Deathtoucher are already distinguishable — just not on the board. This is plumbing, not research.

The architecture, adapted

Mapping the ByteRL-style design onto MTG. Teal is machinery we already have; rust is what would have to be built. The whole missing half sits on the state path — the action path is already the right shape.

Four things to decide together

Shape

Slots or a set?

Largely settled: a set encoder removes the ordering question and generalises past a fixed board. The live sub-question is whether to start with cheap sum-pooling and add attention later, or build attention once — combat is relational, and pooling destroys relations.

Ordering

What order do creatures go in?

Moot under a set encoder — permutation invariance by construction, so W0Twin has nothing to catch. It returns the moment we use fixed ordered slots instead, which is the cheaper build.

Hand

Is hand size really enough?

Rung 0 has no tricks, so their hand may genuinely not matter. My hand decides whether a trade is cheap or ruinous, and right now it's one integer.

Order of work

Reference first?

A richer encoding produces no measurable change while the referee is one combat deep. That argues the k-turn reference lands first — but it's the slower build, and you may want the encoding ready to test against it.
